LinkedIn Publishing From a Single-Founder Local Rust App: Options, Capability Matrix and Verification (as of 30 Oct 2026)

Bottom line: For the personal profile, use Option A: the self-serve “Share on LinkedIn” product with w_member_social, calling the versioned REST Posts API. LinkedIn’s own docs list w_member_social as sufficient to create text, image, multi-image (2–20), PDF document, poll, video and article posts. flaghand The company page is a different case. Every route to automated company-page posting (Option B, Community Management API) is gated: it is limited to “registered legal organizations for commercial use cases only”, needs a verified business email, and must sit on a dedicated app with no other products. microsoftmicrosoft So use D (manual paste) for the page today, and apply for B’s Development tier only if you have a registered entity. Option C (365-day refresh tokens) is not realistically available to you. microsoftmicrosoft The cost is a browser re-consent at least every 60 days, which takes only a few minutes a year.

TL;DR


Personal profile → A. The w_member_social scope can publish all the post formats you need, including PDF carousels (100 MB / 300 pages; PDF, PPT, PPTX, DOC, DOCX) and multi-image posts (2–20 images). flaghand +2 Analytics, reading comments and listing your own posts are not available on A: r_member_social is “closed”, and r_member_postAnalytics comes only with the vetted Community Management API. microsoftmicrosoft
Company page → D now, B only if you have a registered business. The Community Management API is vetted. Its Development tier allows 500 calls per app and 100 calls per member per 24 h, which is enough for one user. It must be the only product on its app, and LinkedIn approves “at its discretion”. microsoftmicrosoft B is also the only official route to post analytics (personal or page).
Tokens: access tokens last 60 days. Programmatic 365-day refresh tokens are for “a limited set of partners” and approved MDP partners. microsoftmicrosoft Plan on re-running the OAuth browser flow before expiry, about 6–7 times a year at 1–2 minutes each. On the version header: 202602 is still Active until 15 Feb 2027; target 202610 (Active until 15 Oct 2027). microsoft

What would change the answer (flagged):


Company-page posting needs an approval a one-person app may not get. This is confirmed [P]. Vetting requires a “registered legal organization”, a “commercial use case”, a verified business email (“Personal email addresses won’t pass”) and an app verified by a page super admin. microsoft A sole founder with a registered company and a domain email can plausibly pass Development-tier vetting. A founder without an entity cannot. PDF posting does not need that approval for the personal profile. The Documents API lists w_member_social, and a person URN may own the document [P]. microsoft However, no first-hand 403/success report for a Share-only app calling /rest/documents was found in Stack Overflow, Microsoft Q&A or GitHub. Only vendor claims and your own March 2026 test support it [S]. One forum report shows a Share-only app getting 403 on /rest reshare and reaction calls [S]. make So test each content type on your app before building on it.
No refresh tokens for a solo developer. This is confirmed as the realistic case [P]. The cost: an interactive browser OAuth run every ≤60 days. If you re-run it while still logged in to linkedin.com and before the token expires, LinkedIn skips the consent screen and redirects straight back [P]. microsoftmicrosoft Estimated cost: ~6–7 runs per year × ~1–2 minutes ≈ 10–15 minutes per year, plus a reminder in the app. The real risk is a missed expiry during a scheduled run. Changing the requested scopes also invalidates all previous tokens [P]. microsoft

Authentication & Tokens


Access token lifetime: 60 days. “Currently, all access tokens are issued with a 60-day lifespan” microsoft (expires_in 5184000). Source: 3-legged OAuth page, ms.date 2025-11-10, last updated 2026-05-15 [P]. microsoft
Refresh tokens: “Programmatic refresh tokens are available for a limited set of partners” [P]. microsoftmicrosoft The Programmatic Refresh Tokens page says LinkedIn “supports programmatic refresh tokens for all approved Marketing Developer Platform (MDP) partners”. Refresh tokens last 365 days, and refreshing does not extend that TTL [P, page date not captured]. microsoft A Microsoft Q&A answer from Aug 2026 also says approval is required and refresh is not a portal toggle [S]. microsoft
Re-consent without refresh tokens: “To refresh an access token, go through the authorization process again.” microsoft If the member is logged in to linkedin.com and the current token has not expired, “the authorization screen is bypassed” [P]. Requesting a different scope invalidates all previous tokens [P]. microsoft
PKCE / native desktop: LinkedIn documents a separate native flow at /oauth/native-pkce/authorization with S256 and a loopback redirect (example http://127.0.0.1:3000). It requires the system browser, not a webview. It is not self-serve: “please reach out to your point of contact at LinkedIn, and we will enable PKCE OAuth 2 flow for your app” [P, page date not captured]. microsoft Developers report that the standard /oauth/v2/authorization endpoint rejects a code_verifier with a confidential client (“invalid_client”) and returns “Not enough permissions to access Native PKCE protocol” when native PKCE isn’t enabled [S: GitHub PRs/issues, 2025–2026]. github +2
Practical local-app pattern: use the standard confidential authorization-code flow with client_secret stored in the OS keychain (single user, own app). Note the risk: anyone with your binary/config could extract the secret. The docs say the redirect URL is added “via HTTPS” and must be absolute, without # [P]. microsoft Whether http://localhost is accepted on the standard flow is not found in primary docs. Native PKCE explicitly uses http://127.0.0.1 loopback [P]. microsoft Fallbacks: the Developer Portal Token Generator (manual token, 60 days) [P], or an https:// redirect you control that hands the code back to the local app. microsoft

Exclusivity rule: “The Community Management API requires that it be the only product associated with a developer application due to legal and security constraints.” With any other product (e.g., Share) or a pending request, the request button is greyed out [P, Partner Support Guide]. microsoft Implication: run two apps, one Share app for personal posting and one Community Management app. The Community Management API itself also bundles w_member_social, so an approved Community Management app could serve both surfaces plus analytics. microsoft
Development tier limits: 500 calls/app/24 h; 100 calls/member/24 h; BATCH_GET not allowed; social-action webhooks disabled. You are “expected to build core business use cases … within twelve (12) months” [P, Increasing Access 2026-10-19]. microsoft What happens after 12 months on the Development tier is not found.
Discretion: “LinkedIn reserves the right to review applications and select partners at its discretion and a partner might not be upgraded even if they meet these minimum requirements” [P]. Restricted APIs have “no fixed timeline” [P]. microsoft
Typical time to approval: not found in primary sources for the Community Management API. The “2–16 weeks” estimate is unverified.
Rejection reasons: documented review criteria are approved use case, verified business email, verified organization, website/domain, and page verification [P]. microsoft A reported failure mode is “Identity vetting failed — unable to verify that your organization is a legally registered, active entity” for an LLP incorporated 2 June 2026 and not yet in third-party databases, with appeals auto-rejected [S, Microsoft Q&A 2026]. microsoft Rejected applicants must create a new app to reapply [P]. microsoft
Own-page-only app: the review screencast criteria are written for a “Page Management” use case (a user posting to their LinkedIn page), so a single-company self-management tool fits the use-case list [P]. microsoft No doc says single-user apps are excluded or approved. Not found.

Terms / Policy


User Agreement §8.2 (Don’ts): “Use bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services, … create, comment on, like, share, or re-share posts, or otherwise drive inauthentic engagement” [P, User Agreement, effective date not captured]. linkedin Using LinkedIn’s official API through an approved product is authorized automation. Browser automation or scraping of linkedin.com is not. profilespiderlinkedin
Developer AI Policy (last updated 2026-01-14) [P]: developers should “Restrict automated posting of AI-generated content to external sites (including LinkedIn) … without end user involvement”. If generated content is attributed to the user, “the end user should be provided tools to first edit the generated content”. Users must be told when they interact with AI (“AI-powered” label) and that output may be inaccurate. microsoft Your human-approval-before-publish design satisfies the core requirement. The policy formally covers Marketing APIs (e.g., Community Management), but it is good practice for A as well. microsoft
AI labeling / C2PA: Fortune (Jeremy Kahn, 26 Nov 2024) reports that “LinkedIn applies a visible ‘CR’ label in the upper lefthand corner of any content uploaded to its platform that has C2PA content credentials.” Clicking the label shows the creation tool, the signer, and a date and time stamp [S]. LinkedIn Help, as quoted by a secondary source, says: “If you post image or video content that contains C2PA credentials, we will indicate its presence with the C2PA icon.” LinkedIn also says it cannot yet label all AI content [S]. No API field for AI disclosure was found in the Posts schema. Text posts carry no label. lilachbullock If your PDF or images come from AI tools that embed C2PA, expect the badge.
Data storage / use: member social activity data may be stored only 48 h, and most member profile data 24 h. Data may be used only to manage Pages or Profiles. No social-feed use cases [P, Restricted Uses page, older version view 202306; current-version text not re-verified]. microsoft Scraping of LinkedIn is prohibited outside written permission [P, User Agreement / Service Terms]. linkedin
Local single-user relevance: the API Terms say you must not require users to obtain their own access credentials. unt For your own use this is moot.

Caveats


Several primary pages are versioned views (e.g., view 202607) whose “last updated” date can be earlier than the view month. Dates are given as shown.
Vendor blogs that say “it works with w_member_social” have a commercial interest. Treat them as corroboration only.
The Share on LinkedIn product docs still describe legacy v2/ugcPosts. Whether LinkedIn will eventually restrict /rest/* media endpoints for Share-only apps is unknown. Build feature flags per content type and run a smoke test on each new version.
