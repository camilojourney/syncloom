Reading social platforms for topic research, October 2026: official APIs per platform, what is banned, and what to choose

Recommendation: use option E (HN, arXiv, RSS and news) as the base, add official read APIs only where they are free or cost a few dollars (Bluesky, YouTube, Hacker News, a capped X budget, and Threads if App Review passes), and add option D for the founder’s own metrics. Reject B (polite fetching of public pages) and C (licensed providers). Most platforms do not ban personal research reads outright. Instead they either price them cheaply (X), offer them free (Bluesky, YouTube, HN, Threads), or close them to individuals (Reddit as of 31 Oct 2026, LinkedIn, TikTok, Facebook public search). That mixed picture triggers neither “would change the answer” condition across the board.

TL;DR


Workable official read paths for one person:

Bluesky: open, free firehose and public AppView.
Hacker News: Firebase API with no rate limit, plus Algolia search at 10,000 requests per hour per IP.
YouTube: free. Since 1 June 2026, search.list has its own bucket of 100 calls a day.
X: pay-per-use at $0.005 per post read, $0.001 per owned read and $0.010 per trends request. Recent search covers the last 7 days.
Threads: keyword and tag search, limited to 2,200 queries per rolling 24 hours, after App Review.



Closed or effectively closed to a non-academic individual:

Reddit: new public Data API requests stop 31 Oct 2026, RSS ends 13 Nov 2026, and remaining public access ends March 2027.
LinkedIn: no public post search. Member post data may be cached for at most 48 hours. Scraping is barred by contract.
TikTok: the Research API is for academics and non-profits only.
Facebook and Instagram research: Meta Content Library requires an academic or not-for-profit research affiliation. A solo founder does not qualify on any platform’s researcher program.



Scraping is prohibited by contract on most platforms, and enforcement is real but legally mixed. X lost its contract claims against Bright Data in 2024 on copyright-preemption grounds. On 31 Jul 2026, Judge Paul Engelmayer (SDNY) let Reddit’s DMCA anti-circumvention claims against SerpApi and Perplexity proceed, dismissing only the trafficking, unfair competition and unjust enrichment claims. X sent cease-and-desist letters to Nitter on 24 Aug 2026, citing “unlawful use and circumvention of X’s Application Programming Interface (API)” (TechCrunch, 25 Aug 2026). Licensed providers (option C) are quote-based enterprise products. The cheap self-serve ones are scraping-based. Neither fits “a few dollars a month.”

Details

X (Twitter)


Pricing (primary). https://docs.x.com/x-api/getting-started/pricing (undated; read 30 Oct 2026):

“The X API uses pay-per-usage pricing… No subscriptions.” Posts Read costs $0.005 per resource, User Read $0.010, Trends $0.010 per request, and Counts Recent $0.005 per request. x
Owned Reads cost “$0.001 per resource (1,000 resources for $1)”. This applies to GET /2/users/{id}/tweets and /mentions when the authenticated user owns the app. x
Resources are deduplicated within a 24-hour UTC window. This is a “soft guarantee.” x
Spending limits are configurable. x



Monthly read cap conflict. The pricing page says “capped at 3 million Post reads per monthly billing cycle.” x https://docs.x.com/x-api/fundamentals/post-cap says “a monthly cap of 2 million Post reads.” x The pricing page is probably authoritative, but this is unresolved. It does not matter at founder scale.
Search. https://docs.x.com/x-api/posts/search/introduction (undated) says recent search covers “Posts from the last 7 days. Available to all developers.” It also says “Full-archive search is available to pay-per-use and Enterprise customers.” x

Basic and Pro are no longer offered to new developers. On 6 Feb 2026, @XDevelopers posted “Officially launching X API Pay-Per-Use,” saying legacy Free users “will move to Pay-Per-Use with a one-time $10 voucher.”



AI. https://docs.x.com/developer-terms/restricted-use-cases (undated): “X prohibits any use of the X APIs and/or X Content to fine-tune or train a foundation or frontier model with the exception of Grok.” x

The page also bans “investigating or tracking X users or their content.” x
Using posts as prompt input for inference is not expressly addressed: NF. Feeding only aggregated topic signals to a model is the defensible reading, not storing raw posts in prompts.



Enforcement.

X Corp. v. Bright Data (N.D. Cal., Judge Alsup) was dismissed in May 2024. The court held X’s contract claims over scraping and selling public data were preempted by the Copyright Act (2°: Proskauer release, 23 May 2024; casemine). proskauercasemine
X sent cease-and-desist letters to Nitter on 24 Aug 2026, accusing it of “unlawful use and circumvention of X’s Application Programming Interface (API) and associated data” and giving it until 5 p.m. EST on 25 Aug to shut down (TechCrunch, Sarah Perez, 25 Aug 2026). Nitter briefly came back in early September, and its GitHub repository was archived again on 11 Sep 2026 (TechCrunch, 15 Sep 2026).



Cost at founder scale. About 2,000 third-party post reads a month is $10. Owned reads for your own posts and mentions cost cents. Thirty trends requests cost $0.30.

Reddit


Primary. https://support.reddithelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/54353370049684-Changelog-October-8-2026 (8 Oct 2026):

“Reddit will stop supporting RSS feeds… November 13, 2026… If you use RSS for feeds outside of a community you moderate, there is no replacement.” reddithelp
“public Data API apps and bots are moving to Reddit’s Developer Platform as part of efforts to reduce scraping and automated abuse.” reddithelp



Dates (Reddit admin post in r/redditdev, 30 Sep 2026, as reported by TechCrunch’s Sarah Perez): new public API requests are not accepted after 31 Oct 2026, unregistered apps lose access from 12 Jan 2027, and remaining public access ends in March 2027. superframeworksberi Registration for the migration bounty closes 30 Nov 2026 (Unite.ai).
Free tier (2°: secondary pages citing the Data API Wiki updated 11 May 2026): 100 queries per minute per OAuth client ID, averaged over 10 minutes, for approved non-commercial use. prowlo

A pre-launch business content studio is arguably commercial use. snitchfeed That is a second reason not to rely on Reddit.



Enforcement. Reddit v. SerpApi LLC et al. (SDNY 1:25-cv-8736, filed 22 Oct 2025): on 31 Jul 2026, Judge Paul A. Engelmayer dismissed only the §1201(b) trafficking claim and the unfair competition and unjust enrichment claims, letting the DMCA anti-circumvention claims proceed (MLex). He found that CAPTCHA and bot detection qualify as access controls “even if that same content is available to human users” (Loeb & Loeb, Aug 2026).

This is the key legal risk for option B. Automated fetching that defeats bot defenses can be framed as circumvention, not just a contract breach.

Options A–E against the constraints


(A) Official read APIs only: viable, but only on about five platforms. Bluesky, HN, YouTube and Threads are free, and X costs a few dollars. Reddit closes tomorrow to new applicants. LinkedIn, TikTok and Facebook give an individual no public-search path.
(B) APIs plus polite fetching of public pages: reject. Scraping is contractually barred on X, LinkedIn and Reddit, and enforcement is escalating (Reddit’s DMCA theory survived dismissal in 2026, and X sent the Nitter cease-and-desist). Bright Data’s wins rested on contract preemption and logged-out scraping, not on a right to bypass bot defenses. elgrith Logged-in browser automation is the worst case, because the account holder is bound by the ToS. lobstr
(C) Licensed provider: reject on price. Licensed vendors are quote-based, with Vendr medians from about $13.3k a year (Talkwalker) to $50,000 a year (Brandwatch), per Octolens (Sep 2026). Affordable APIs are scraping-based, which simply moves option B’s risk onto a vendor.
(D) Own posts, comments and metrics: adopt. It is cheapest and cleanest. X owned reads cost $0.001 each, YouTube authorized statistics may be kept with a re-authorization check every 30 days, and LinkedIn organization analytics and Threads insights need app approval. github
(E) News, RSS, HN and arXiv: adopt as the base. It has no ToS friction for topic discovery and is free. HN’s Algolia API alone gives keyword, trend and comment-thread signals. dev
Would-change conditions.

Personal reads are neither banned nor overpriced on most platforms; about half are open and cheap. So E does not win outright, but it carries most of the weight.
The only clear non-commercial permission was Reddit’s, and it closes to new applicants on 31 Oct 2026. Researcher programs (Meta, TikTok) exclude non-academic individuals.





Recommended combination: E + D + a narrow A.


Ingest HN (Algolia plus Firebase), arXiv, news RSS and Bluesky Jetstream filtered to keywords.
Pull your own metrics on X (owned reads), YouTube Analytics and Threads insights.
Add X recent search with a hard monthly spending limit of about $5 to $10, YouTube search.list (100 a day) and Threads keyword search if App Review passes.
In Rust, store only derived topic aggregates (term counts, URLs, IDs). Expire any raw text within 24 to 48 hours, which is the strictest of the limits above (LinkedIn). Honor YouTube’s 30-day rule and Bluesky deletion events.
Never use platform content to fine-tune a model.


Caveats and open questions


X’s monthly cap conflicts (2M vs 3M) across its own docs. X’s rules on inference and prompt input for LLMs: NF.
Reddit’s Data API Terms and Wiki text and the exact closure timing were read only through secondary sources. Whether a Devvit app could serve personal research: NF.
The full Bluesky Terms of Service on third-party AI use of public data: NF. The searchPosts and trending endpoints were not verified in primary docs.
Primary Instagram and Facebook insights docs, the Meta Platform Terms clause on AI and scraping, the TikTok and YouTube ToS scraping clauses, YouTube channel RSS, and HN’s terms: NF in this research.
LinkedIn §8.2, hiQ, Meta v. Bright Data, X v. Bright Data and Reddit v. SerpApi are all summarized from secondary legal commentary. Have counsel confirm them before relying on them.
Whether a pre-launch company studio counts as “non-commercial” is a legal judgment. Assume it is commercial.
